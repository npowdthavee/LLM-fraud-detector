# 1. Background and rationale

The purpose of this small validation study is to establish, before preregistration of a separate AI financial-advice experiment, whether 14 hypothetical investment scenarios are perceived by finance-experienced adults in the intended way. The scenarios are designed to span clearly legitimate investments, high-risk but legitimate investments, and opportunities containing strong scam/fraud indicators. Independent validation reduces the risk that scenario classifications are imposed solely by the research team.

The validation responses will be used only to determine whether each scenario meets prespecified acceptance criteria or requires revision before the later confirmatory AI study. Individual-level validation responses are not intended to serve as substantive outcome data in the later paper.

# 2. Objectives

- Obtain independent ratings of perceived legitimacy, financial risk, scam/fraud red flags, and investment advisability for each of 14 hypothetical investment opportunities.
- Obtain an overall categorical classification of each opportunity as legitimate, high-risk but legitimate, scam/fraud-indicative, or unclear.
- Use prespecified validation thresholds to decide whether each scenario is retained, revised, or replaced before preregistration of the subsequent AI experiment.

# 3. Study design

Cross-sectional, anonymous, minimal-risk online survey. All participants evaluate the same 14 hypothetical scenarios. Scenario order is randomized for each participant. The questions within each scenario are presented in a fixed order. There is no deception, intervention, feedback manipulation, or collection of sensitive personal information.

# 4. Participants and eligibility

Participants will be recruited through Prolific using prescreening filters. The study will target adults with a finance-related background. Eligibility criteria are:

- Age 21 years or older.
- Able to read and respond in English fluently.
- Meets the Prolific finance-related prescreen selected by the research team (e.g., finance expertise/industry/field filter available on the platform at launch).
- Provides electronic informed consent before beginning the survey.

Broad professional-background questions (years of finance-related experience, primary area, and relevant qualifications) will be collected for descriptive documentation of validator expertise. These questions will use broad categories only and will not request employer names, names of institutions, job titles in free text, names, email addresses, telephone numbers, or other direct identifiers.

# 5. Recruitment

Recruitment will occur through Prolific. The advertisement will identify the project as NTU research, describe the task as evaluating hypothetical investment opportunities, provide eligibility criteria, estimated duration and compensation, and include the approved NTU-IRB project reference and contact details once assigned. The advertisement will not emphasize payment or claim direct benefit.

# 6. Procedures

1. Participants open the Qualtrics survey from Prolific.
2. The first page provides study information, confirms age eligibility, and obtains electronic consent. Participants who do not consent do not proceed to study questions.
3. Participants answer three broad finance-background questions.
4. Participants evaluate 14 hypothetical investment scenarios in randomized order. For each scenario they rate legitimacy (0–100), financial risk (0–100), scam/fraud red flags (0–100), and advisability of investing $10,000 (0–100), then select one overall category.
5. At survey completion participants receive the Prolific completion code/redirect and are compensated through Prolific.

# 7. Scenario-validation measures

| Construct | Response | Anchors |
|---|---|---|
| Legitimacy | 0–100 | 0 = clearly illegitimate/fraudulent; 100 = clearly legitimate |
| Financial risk | 0–100 | 0 = very low risk; 100 = extremely high risk |
| Scam/fraud red flags | 0–100 | 0 = no red flags; 100 = very strong red flags |
| Advisability | 0–100 | 0 = strongly advise against; 100 = strongly advise proceeding |
| Overall classification | Single choice | Legitimate; High-risk but legitimate; Scam/fraud-indicative; Unclear/insufficient information |

# 8. Prespecified scenario acceptance criteria

| Intended category | Acceptance criteria |
|---|---|
| Legitimate | At least 4/5 validators classify as Legitimate; median legitimacy ≥75; median scam/fraud-red-flag score ≤25. |
| High-risk but legitimate | At least 4/5 validators classify as High-risk but legitimate; median legitimacy ≥75; median financial-risk score ≥70; median scam/fraud-red-flag score ≤30. |
| Scam/fraud-indicative | At least 4/5 validators classify as Scam/fraud-indicative; median legitimacy ≤30; median scam/fraud-red-flag score ≥70. |

Advisability will be summarized but will not define scenario category, because an investment can be legitimate yet still be a poor choice. A scenario that fails its intended criteria will be revised or replaced based on expert feedback/ratings and revalidated before the subsequent preregistration. The LLM pilot results will not be used to tune scenario content at this stage.

# 9. Risks and discomforts

The study involves no more than minimal risk. Participants evaluate fictional investment descriptions and provide professional judgments. No questions ask about the participant’s own finances, losses, scams, health, legal matters, employer, or other sensitive personal experiences. The main foreseeable burden is mild fatigue from evaluating 14 scenarios. Participants may stop at any time before submitting their response.

# 10. Benefits

There is no expected direct personal benefit. The study may improve the methodological quality of subsequent research on AI-generated financial advice by ensuring that its hypothetical investment scenarios are independently validated before use.

# 11. Compensation

Participants who complete the survey will receive £6.25 for an estimated 25-minute survey (equivalent to approximately £15/hour). Payment will be administered through Prolific. Participants who decline consent will not be enrolled in the study and will follow the platform’s approved no-consent completion path.

# 12. Consent process

Electronic informed consent will be obtained on the first Qualtrics page. Because the survey is anonymous, no signature, typed name, email address, or other identifying information will be collected. The application will request waiver of written documentation of consent, not waiver of the consent process itself. Participants indicate consent by selecting “I have read the information above, confirm that I am at least 21 years old, and agree to participate” before continuing.

# 13. Privacy, confidentiality and data handling

- Qualtrics will be configured to anonymize responses and not record IP address, location data, names, email addresses, Prolific IDs, or other direct identifiers in the research dataset.
- No participant-specific identifier will be passed from Prolific into Qualtrics. Prolific will administer recruitment/payment separately; the study team will not create a linkage file between Prolific account identifiers and survey responses.
- Survey data will be exported to access-controlled NTU-approved institutional storage (e.g., NTU OneDrive/SharePoint) accessible only to the PI and authorized study team members.
- Research data will be retained for 10 years after publication or project completion, whichever is later, consistent with NTU research-data policy.
- Because the sample is small, individual-level validation responses will not be publicly posted. Only aggregate validation decisions or summary statistics may be referred to in research documentation or the later manuscript.

# 14. Data analysis

Analysis is descriptive and rule-based. For each scenario, the study team will calculate medians for the four 0–100 ratings and the number/proportion of validators selecting each category. These values will be compared with the prespecified acceptance criteria in Section 8. No participant-level inferential hypothesis testing is planned for this validation study.

# 15. Withdrawal and deletion

Participants may stop the survey before submission. Because no identifying information or participant-specific survey ID will be retained, once an anonymous response has been submitted it will generally not be possible to identify and delete a particular participant’s response.

# 16. Dissemination

The validation exercise is preparatory to a separate AI financial-advice study. Individual-level expert-validation data are not intended to be reported as substantive outcomes in that paper. The manuscript may state that scenarios were independently expert-validated and may report aggregate validation criteria/results if methodologically necessary. No participant will be identifiable in any report or publication.
